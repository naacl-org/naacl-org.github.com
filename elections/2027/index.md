<a name="Helena Gómez Adorno"> </a>

### [Helena Gómez Adorno](https://helenagomez-adorno.github.io)

#### Bio
Helena Gómez-Adorno is a Researcher at the Research Institute of Applied Mathematics and Systems (IIMAS) at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM). She received her Ph.D. in Computer Science from the National Polytechnic Institute (México) in 2018, where she introduced graph-based text representations for various natural language processing tasks. Her Ph.D. thesis was awarded for outstanding academic performance a doctoral student. More recently, she was awarded the 2026 Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz Recognition by UNAM for her academic contributions. Her expertise led her to publications on topics related to text classification for authorship analysis, fake news detection, clinical NLP, and hate speech detection, among others. Helena has been actively involved in the NLP community, serving as co-program chair for NAACL 2024 and and Co-Chair of the Diversity and Inclusion Committee for EACL 2026. She has participated as a Reviewer, Area Chair, and Senior Area Chair for NAACL conferences since 2019. Additionally, she has served on the organizing committee of the Mexican NLP Summer School in 2020, 2021, and 2024. She is currently an elected member of the NAACL Board for the 2025-2026 term. 

#### Candidacy Statement
I am pleased to present my candidacy for reelection to the NAACL Board. As a researcher from Latin America, affiliated with universities in Mexico and Paraguay, I remain committed to building a more inclusive and representative NLP community across the Americas. Researchers in the Global South face challenges related to access to computational resources, research funding, conference participation, and institutional publishing requirements. These differences shape our research opportunities, and I believe NAACL can play an important role in reducing these barriers.

Having served on the NAACL Board during 2025–2026, I would like to continue contributing to the chapter's mission of representing the diverse NLP communities across North, Central, and South America. If reelected, I would focus on three priorities:

**Strengthening Latin American participation and representation.**  I would continue advocating for greater involvement of Latin American researchers in NAACL organizing committees and leadership roles. I would also support regional workshops, satellite events, mentorship programs, and funding opportunities that help researchers from underrepresented regions participate more actively in our community.

**Promoting diversity and broader research perspectives.** I would support initiatives such as WiNLP, student workshops, LatinX in AI, and Queer in AI, while encouraging greater visibility for research addressing the linguistic, cultural, and societal challenges of the Americas. I also believe that stronger connections between academia and industry in Latin America could create valuable opportunities for collaboration, mentorship, and regional research development.

**Improving conference organization.** Drawing on my experience as Co-Program Chair of NAACL 2024 in Mexico City, I would advocate for clearer guidelines, reusable organizational resources, and better coordination between conference programming and registration processes. 

The transition to The Nations of the Americas Chapter represents an important commitment to the entire continent. I believe our next step should be to turn this commitment into more concrete opportunities for participation, collaboration, and representation. If reelected, I would be glad to continue contributing to this effort.


<a name="David Jurgens"> </a>

### [David Jurgens](https://jurgens.people.si.umich.edu)

#### Bio
David Jurgens is an Associate Professor in the School of Information and the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan. His research studies social intelligence in humans and machines through language. He was a Program Chair for ACL 2026, Tutorials Chair for ACL 2025, co-organized Midwest Speech and Language Days in 2023, and has worked on revisions to the responsible NLP checklist for ACL 2026 and ACL Rolling Review. He has regularly served as Senior Area Chair and Area Chair for *CL venues over the past decade. His research has been supported by the NSF (including a CAREER), NIH, IARPA, OpenAI, and Amazon, among others. His work has won the Cozzarelli Prize from the National Academy of Science, the Cialdini Prize from the Society for Personality and Social Psychology, best paper awards and nominations at ACL, ICWSM, Web Science, and Social Informatics.

#### Candidacy Statement
If elected, I will focus on three areas: (1) bringing researchers together closer to where they work, (2) grounding changes to reviewing in evidence, and (3) connecting NAACL to the neighboring fields that study language.

**Satellite sites and regional events.**  A first-time attendee at an *ACL conference can find it hard to meet and talk with other researchers, and travel budgets and visa issues influence who can even attend in person. I will work to increase opportunities for networking while lowering the cost of participation, with two aims. First, I will propose satellite sites for the main conference across the Americas where remote participants gather in person, present, and network with each other. Second, I will work to give regional events NAACL support and visibility through a shared organizing handbook and a common calendar. Events such as Midwest Speech and Language Days, SoCal NLP, and SouthNLP build community in their regions at a fraction of the travel cost and bring together researchers who usually attend different conferences. Regional events can also run in local languages. Lowering the cost of participation will strengthen and diversify our community.

**Improving reviewing with evidence.** I want reviewing to be efficient and effective for authors and reviewers, and I have pursued that goal by studying our own processes and changing those processes in response. As an ACL 2026 Program Chair, I revised the ARR checklist for the conference. After ACL 2026, I analyzed who reviews for ACL and who is qualified to review, and the analysis helped inform how ARR now recruits reviewers. I then served on a special committee that further streamlined the checklist for the current ARR cycle. As a board member, I will continue this work. For example, I will pursue two changes. First, I will work to streamline AI-use disclosure so that it focuses on the information the community values. Second, I will propose a two-phased review where papers may be initially rejected by an AC or SAC and receive a detailed rationale. A rejection of this kind still gives authors feedback on what to improve while saving the effort of three reviewers to put towards papers more likely to be accepted. I will work with ARR to test changes like these alongside live reviewing without changing any decisions, as NeurIPS did with its consistency experiments.

**Co-location with neighboring fields.** Our community studies language from many directions, and machine learning work is welcome alongside linguistic, cognitive, and social approaches. Bridging disciplines leads to productive conversations, like the co-location of SCiL with ACL 2026 showed. I will pursue NAACL co-locations with other language-focused communities, such as CogSci, the LSA Annual Meeting, NWAV, and IC2S2; and I aim to co-locate NAACL (or its satellites) with Latin American venues such as SIMBig in Peru and STIL in Brazil, the latter of which already appears in the ACL Anthology.



<a name="Manuel Mager"> </a>

### [Manuel Mager](http://code.kiutz.com)

#### Bio
Manuel Mager is an Associate Professor at Universidad Iberoamericana in Mexico City and a co-founder and organizer of AmericasNLP. His research focuses primarily on natural language processing for indigenous and polysynthetic languages, along with LLM safety and graph-to-text generation. He holds a Ph.D. in Natural Sciences from the University of Stuttgart, Germany. Previously, he served as an Acting Professor at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz and worked as an Applied Scientist at Amazon. He has served as a Senior Area Chair (SAC), Ethics Chair, and Diversity & Inclusion Chair for NAACL conferences, and is currently an ARR Ethics Co-Chair.

#### Candidacy Statement
NAACL is facing a set of challenges in the era of LLMs and fast-paced research. The center of development has shifted toward language modeling and has concentrated in the Global North and big tech companies. These actors are important and central to our community, but expanding inclusion further south is equally vital. In this context, our association should provide strong support for alternative views, promoting a stronger integration of all regions that NAACL covers (including LATAM and the Caribbean) and directly tackling the specific challenges these regions face. Therefore, I aim to support:

**A new regional journal venue:** The South of the Americas still relies heavily on journals as its most renowned venues. We can build bridges with a new regional journal where researchers can close the gap between the North and the South.

**Special tracks for alternative paradigms:** Special tracks that promote the publication of alternative models will encourage higher-risk research, allowing researchers to break away from the "publish LLMs or perish" mentality.

**Support ARR and expand our reviewer pool to the south:** Starting an active campaign to engage researchers from Global South institutes and labs will strengthen our ARR reviewer pool.

**Support and promote cultural and linguistic diversity:** As our continent is rich in cultural and linguistic diversity, NAACL should continue and strengthen these aspects.



<a name="Adriana Pagano"> </a>

### [Adriana Pagano](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@adriana-s-pagano)

#### Bio
Adriana Pagano is Full Professor at the Federal University of Minas Gerais (UFMG), Brazil. Originally from Argentina, she holds undergraduate degrees in Language studies and translation from Universidad Nacional de La Plata (UNLP). She built her academic career in Brazil, with research and teaching appointments in Europe, Asia, and North America. Her work connects applied linguistics, Systemic Functional Linguistics, and translation studies with computational linguistics and responsible AI.

She leads international interdisciplinary projects spanning linguistics, computer science, healthcare, law, and environmental studies. Currently, these include the Worldwide Universities Network project "Algorithms for Fair Representation: Debiasing Large Language Models with Culturally-Diverse Datasets" and CLAReIA, which addresses accessible language, representation, and environmental equity. Her research focuses on multilingual resources, linguistic annotation, cultural adaptation, and language technology evaluation.

She is a founding member of Brasileiras em PLN (Brazilian Women in Natural Language Processing) and serves on the ACL Ethics Committee. Her service includes university governance, research network development, and interdisciplinary teaching and mentoring.


#### Candidacy Statement
I am honored to stand for election to the NAACL Board. As an applied linguist from Argentina with an academic career built in Brazil and sustained international collaborations, I would contribute a Latin American perspective and experience leading interdisciplinary projects. My approach draws on "appliable linguistics": a socially accountable framework in which theory and practice continually inform one another.

If elected, I would pursue four priorities.

**Strengthening the "L" in computational linguistics.**
I would promote exchange between computational methods and linguistic accounts of meaning, structure, variation, and use, and advocate for recognition of linguistic description, annotated resources, and theoretically informed analysis. This includes attention to Indigenous languages and diversity within widely spoken languages, with Indigenous communities guiding priorities and decisions about their language data.

**Supporting interdisciplinary collaboration.**
Drawing on my project leadership in healthcare, law, and environmental studies, I would support workshops and shared tasks where NLP researchers, domain specialists, and community partners jointly define research questions and evaluation criteria. I would advocate for conference programming and review expertise that welcome interdisciplinary contributions and encourage exchanges among academia, industry, and other sectors.

**Building community and expanding training across the Americas.**
I would prioritize initiatives that reach countries and institutions with limited access to NLP training and international research networks. Working with local organizers, I would support regional schools, affordable workshops, and remote training and mentoring in linguistic analysis, computational methods, academic writing, and reviewing. Multilingual educational materials  and activities designed for different time zones and connectivity conditions could help reduce barriers for students and early-career researchers. I would also strengthen connections among NAACL activities, regional networks, and initiatives such as ACL mentoring, WiNLP, and Brasileiras em PLN, ensuring that communities with fewer resources help shape these opportunities.

**Using resources responsibly and sustainably.**
I would encourage transparent reporting of computational costs, recognition of meaningful advances achieved with modest resources, and reuse of datasets, tools, and educational materials. In NAACL activities, I would support participation options that balance in-person exchange with remote access, considering affordability, environmental impacts, and volunteer workloads.

My goal is a NAACL that people across North, Central, and South America can participate in and help shape: useful to academia and industry, open to different research traditions, and a place where linguists and machine learning researchers learn from one another. I would work toward this through collaborative decision-making and practical initiatives developed with the community.


<a name="Francisco (Paco) Guzmán"> </a>

### [Francisco (Paco) Guzmán](https://guzmanhe.github.io/)

#### Bio
I'm Francisco (Paco) Guzmán, Head of AI Research at Handshake. I lead research on data quality and evaluation; creating data and systems that expand the knowledge and reliability of frontier AI through expert data. Previously at Meta, I led efforts like NLLB-200 (expanding translation to 200 languages and billions of speakers) and SeamlessM4T (recognized by TIME as one of 2023's best inventions), pushing multilingual and open-science work that expanded language technology access worldwide. I also led post-training teams for Llama 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3; advancing open-weights models that empower researchers globally.

I created the AI Learning Alliance (AILA) with Georgia Tech, partnering with HBCUs, HSIs to democratize AI education; co-created the TICO-19 initiative to make COVID-19 information accessible in 35+ languages; organized the first WMT shared task on African languages; and have served as Co-Research Director at AMTA (2020-2022), Ethics Co-Chair at EMNLP 2023, and Area Chair at conferences including NeurIPS, ACL, and EMNLP.  I'm deeply committed to mentorship and to building research ecosystems in industry and academia that scale inclusion and provide opportunities for everyone.

#### Candidacy Statement
NAACL is where rigor, openness, and inclusivity converge. I'm running for the Board to strengthen those pillars by investing in three areas:

**Compute access fund:** Access to compute has become the major inequalizer in research. Smaller universities across the Americas, particularly in Latin America, cannot compete when frontier research requires resources only industry can afford. I will establish a NAACL Compute Fund in partnership with cloud providers (e.g., AWS, Google Cloud, Azure), making grants available to researchers at institutions without significant compute budgets. This fund will lower the entrance bar so more researchers in the Americas can conduct cutting-edge research and remain competitive in the AI era. Success will be measured by: number of institutions served, papers published using the grants, and geographic distribution across the hemisphere.

**Evaluation standards and reproducibility:** With training data contamination threatening benchmark validity, establish lightweight, NAACL-endorsed rubrics for dataset quality, reproducibility, and contamination checks. This raises the floor for fairness and transparency in our review and benchmark culture, protecting research integrity while making good science easier to verify.

**Mentorship and regional collaboration:** Scale mentorship programs connecting students with experienced researchers from academia and industry. Establish a NAACL Research Apprenticeship Program that provides grants for paid internships at leading labs across the Americas; targeting not only NLP students but also researchers from other disciplines (Biology, Physics, Chemistry, Social Sciences) to foster interdisciplinary AI research addressing real-world problems across language barriers. Support regional workshops and shared tasks that strengthen our continental identity; ensuring researchers have platforms to showcase work on problems unique to the Americas.

NAACL's strength lies in its people and its standards. My goal is to help the organization set both higher and fairer; so good science is easier to do and verify across the Americas. I commit to substantial time for board work and championing transparency in our publication guidelines.
